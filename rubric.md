# Click-to-Activation Audit Rubric v0

Use this rubric to evaluate how well a landing page moves a visitor from first click toward activation. Score each category from 1 to 5.

## 1. Clarity of Value Proposition

- A 5 means the page makes the product, target user, and primary benefit obvious within seconds.
- A 3 means the general offering is understandable, but the message is somewhat vague, overloaded, or slow to land.
- A 1 means the visitor is likely confused about what the product is, who it is for, or why it matters.
- Common failure modes: vague headlines, jargon-heavy copy, unclear audience, buried outcome, too many competing messages.

## 2. Trust and Credibility

- A 5 means the page feels credible, professional, and safe to engage with, with strong proof or reassuring context.
- A 3 means the page has some trust signals, but important credibility gaps remain.
- A 1 means the page feels risky, thin, unproven, or potentially unprofessional.
- Common failure modes: no proof points, weak or absent social proof, vague claims, thin company context, poor polish, mismatch between promise and presentation.

## 3. CTA Clarity and Motivation

- A 5 means the primary call to action is easy to find, easy to understand, and compelling relative to the visitor's intent.
- A 3 means the CTA exists and is visible, but the motivation, wording, or hierarchy is only moderately effective.
- A 1 means the visitor is unclear on what to do next, why they should do it, or which action matters most.
- Common failure modes: generic button copy, too many CTAs, hidden primary action, weak reason to click, poor CTA-to-message alignment.

## 4. Friction and Confusion

- A 5 means the page minimizes uncertainty, answers obvious objections, and reduces cognitive load before the next step.
- A 3 means the page is usable but leaves avoidable ambiguity, unanswered questions, or distracting complexity.
- A 1 means the page creates significant hesitation, confusion, or mental effort that likely suppresses conversion.
- Common failure modes: cluttered layout, missing explanations, inconsistent messaging, distracting sections, unclear process, unresolved objections.

## 5. Onboarding Momentum

- A 5 means the page clearly sets up what happens after the click and makes initial activation feel fast, safe, and worthwhile.
- A 3 means there is some path forward, but the first-use experience or post-click expectation is not concrete enough.
- A 1 means the transition from click to activation feels uncertain, heavy, or poorly motivated.
- Common failure modes: no explanation of next steps, unclear setup burden, weak activation framing, hidden time-to-value, abrupt handoff after the CTA.

## Analysis Instructions

- Prioritize specificity over generic advice.
- Do not invent analytics or conversion data.
- Do not assume hidden context.
- Focus on highest-leverage issues.
- Prefer actionable recommendations.
- Avoid spammy or manipulative CRO advice.
- Preserve trust and professionalism for sensitive categories.

## Output Requirements

- Scores for each category.
- Top 3 issues.
- Top 3 recommended fixes.
- 3 quick wins.
- 2 bigger bets.
- A short summary of the likely main conversion problem.
